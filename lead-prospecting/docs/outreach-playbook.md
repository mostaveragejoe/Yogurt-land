# Outreach playbook

Scoring tells you *who* to contact. This tells you *what to say* — and, in one
case, what not to say.

Sequencing note: work the channels in parallel rather than in order. Credit
unions are a long institutional cycle and brokers close fast, so starting the
slow one late costs a quarter. `prospect.py worklist` deliberately groups by
channel to keep several moving at once.

---

## Credit unions — the volume channel

**The angle:** you are not asking for their members. You are offering somewhere
to send the business loans they legally cannot book.

A credit union near its statutory cap already has this problem and hates it —
telling a long-standing member no is the worst part of the job. You are not
asking for a favor; you are solving something.

Open by naming the constraint. `prospect.py show` prints their exact cap
utilization; use the number. It signals immediately that you understand their
business, which is most of the battle in an institutional first contact.

Then name the products they do not do in-house:

| They decline because | We do |
|---|---|
| Not a PLP lender / no SBA department | SBA Loans |
| Credit unions do not lease | Equipment Leasing |
| Too slow for a short-term need | Commercial Bridge |
| Not a credit union product | Accounts Receivable Financing |
| Will not touch it | Fix & Flip |
| Wants three years profitable, goodwill-heavy deal fails | Business Acquisition |
| DSCR/LTV outside policy | Real Estate Financing |
| Will not refinance someone else's mess | Debt Restructuring |

**Expect:** a longer cycle than the other channels — vendor approval, sometimes
a written referral agreement, occasionally legal review. Worth it, because once
signed it produces monthly rather than episodically.

**Target contact:** Chief Lending Officer or VP Commercial/Business Lending. At
under $250M in assets, the CEO.

---

## CPA firms — the quality channel

**Do not open with a referral fee.** AICPA Rule 503 bars it outright for any
client the firm performs audit, review or compilation work for, and no
disclosure cures it. Most small-business CPAs do the compilation and the tax
return for the same client, so for the clients worth referring the fee is
unusable. Leading with commission reads as not understanding their profession.

**The angle instead:** the client they had to tell no. Every CPA has one — the
client whose expansion stalled, whose equipment died, who got declined at the
bank in March. Ask about that client, not about their book.

Offer reciprocal referrals rather than money. It is worth more to them anyway,
and it sidesteps Rule 503 entirely.

**Timing is the constraint.** CPAs are unreachable from January to mid-April.
Seed relationships between May and December or wait a quarter.

**Expect:** the longest courtship of any channel — six to eighteen months to a
producing relationship, and the first question is who else you work with. This
is why the broker channel matters: it produces the proof points that make CPA
outreach efficient.

**Target contact:** Managing Partner at small firms; the partner who owns the
business-client book at larger ones.

---

## Business brokers — the proof-point channel

**The angle:** pure self-interest. Their deal dies without financing and they
do not get paid. There is nothing to overcome.

Lead with speed to term sheet on Business Acquisition and SBA. Ask what their
last deal died on — you will usually get a real answer, because it still stings.

**Expect:** the fastest path to a first funded deal of any channel, in weeks
rather than quarters. Work these first, not because they are the best partners
but because everything else converts better once you can name funded deals.

---

## CRE brokers and fix-and-flip

Same structural motivation as business brokers. Lead with bridge and fix &
flip speed. Real estate investor meetups and REIA groups are a low-friction
place to start — transactional, low trust barrier, no relationship guarding.

---

## Equipment dealers

Pitch a **vendor finance program**, not a referral relationship. The framing
matters: they do not want a lending partner, they want the sale to close, and
financing is the obstacle in front of it.

Given Minnesota's industry mix — 81,000+ farms, heavy construction and
specialty trades, a large trucking and freight base — this channel is
proportionally more valuable here than in most states.

---

## Medical-adjacent

Medical billing companies, practice brokers, dental and veterinary practice
consultants. They see the cash-flow gap before anyone else does, including the
practice owner.

Worth testing early despite the low volume: **Medical Working Capital is a
dedicated line on the product sheet**, and almost nobody competes for these
partners.

## Compliance notes

- **Minnesota has no commercial financing disclosure law** and no broker
  registration requirement — one of roughly 35–40 states with nothing on the
  books. Less restrictive than Missouri. Verify before relying on it; this
  area is moving, with Illinois and New Jersey adding requirements in 2026.
- **AICPA Rule 503** governs every CPA conversation. See above.
- **B2B calling** is largely exempt from federal do-not-call rules, but TCPA
  still applies to cell numbers with prerecorded messages or an autodialer, and
  many small-business "office" lines are mobile. Scrub for wireless before
  anything automated.
- Minnesota maintains its own telemarketing and caller-ID rules; check them
  before any outbound calling campaign.

None of this is legal advice — confirm with counsel before launching outbound.
