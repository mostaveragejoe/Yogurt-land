## Overview

The Time Authority / Mode-Switch system is the scheduling backbone of Hollowdeep: it decides, at every moment, which of two temporal models drives the one shared world — continuous real time (the colony simulation, with pause/1×/2×/3× speed control) or discrete turn-based time (tactics combat). Every simulation system registers with a single manager and is ticked by exactly one active authority; the inactive authority's systems receive nothing, which is why the colony fully pauses during a battle. Switching modes converts no state — the terrain and entities the tactics battle reads are the same live data the colony sim was using a moment earlier — so "your base is the tactics map" is literally true at the data level. The player never sees this system directly; they feel it as instant, trustworthy transitions (breach → battle, battle → colony), responsive speed control, and a world that never desyncs between modes. The architecture, contract, and measured costs are owned by ADR-0001 (Accepted 2026-07-26, spike-validated 61/61); this GDD specifies the design layer at the boundary: what is allowed to happen in each mode, the transition beats, the autosave policy (including the Battle Persistence checkpoint rule — user ruling 2026-08-02), speed-control behavior, and the battle-length budget (CD-9's surviving half).

## Player Fantasy

This system is infrastructure the player never names — but it owns the two things they touch most often and feel hardest: the speed dial and the breach freeze.

**Who holds the clock.** In colony play the clock belongs to the player, and how they hold it is a readout of their confidence. Pause is the drafting table — nothing moves while they think. 1× is supervision. 3× is a statement: *my plan is good enough that I want to watch it run.* Pushing the colony to 3× is the ant-farm reward made into a button; dropping back to pause is the admission that something needs attention. This is Pillar 4 felt from the chair: the colony lives without you, and the dial is what that trust feels like. Pause-planning is a **sanctioned, cost-free Expression window** — stated deliberately, so any future "limits on pause" proposal reads as a pillar violation on sight.

**The test begins.** Then the mountain is breached, and the game takes the clock away. Everything holds still — haulers mid-stride, a mason one swing from finishing the wall — and the game stops asking the player to *plan* and starts asking them to *answer*. Time becomes a queue of turns, and in exchange the player receives the one thing colony play never grants: direct command of individual people. The trade is exact, and it is the whole shape of Pillar 4's bounded exception. The freeze is a **graded moment, never dread**: a prepared player reads it as *show me* — a plan about to be validated; an unprepared player reads exposure — the sick recognition that the west approach is still dirt. That split is Pillar 2 compressed into a single frame, and the presentation must make it legible within about two seconds (a decompression into silence, never a stinger — see Visual/Audio Requirements).

**A battle is a single sitting — and the fight itself cannot be taken back.** The 8–15 minute battle budget (20-minute hard ceiling) and the Battle Persistence rule (user ruling 2026-08-02) are stated here as design, not constraint: you cannot pause the judgment, manually save inside it, or pick it apart across sessions, and quitting suspends rather than cancels — the checkpoint resumes the fight exactly where it stood. What the game guarantees is narrower and more honest than "no rewind exists": **no rewind is reachable cheaply, and no rewind returns you to the same raid.** Manual saving is locked from the raid warning through battle-end, so the newest save you can select entering a fight is always one the game wrote (Rule 9); an older colony save can still be loaded, but it costs real colony time and the raid it rewinds is re-rolled — you retake a different test, you do not replay a known one (CD-20, user ruling 2026-08-07). Two carve-outs are stated rather than hidden: a corrupt checkpoint offers a loud, explicit fallback to the battle-start autosave (disk failure must never brick a colony — ADR-0004 §5), and deleting or editing save files reaches anything — the design closes UI paths, not disks. Players who want the stricter promise can start a colony in **Commitment Mode**: one rolling save, no manual saves, no reload at all (Rule 9). *(This retires and refines CD-GDD-ALIGN M1's caveat in two steps: the checkpoint machinery first delivered "no in-battle scum window" — EC-8 — and CD-20 now states the remaining boundary honestly rather than overclaiming it as absolute.)*

**The clock comes back.** Some minutes later the player gets the dial back — at pause, standing over a hole in a wall they were admiring (the world resumes exactly; the speed does not — the post-battle survey at pause is where scars teach). The wall that broke in the battle is broken in the colony; the colonist who was hauling stone still holds it. There was only ever one world. **Colony time itself is not frozen at the return** (CD-19, user ruling 2026-08-07): it catches up by a small, capped amount owned by Needs & Simulation (#13), run before the player ever sees the survey — the fiction that a fight consumed no time at all was never the promise; "there was only ever one world" is about *data* (position, terrain, what a colonist still holds), not about time standing still while you fought.

**Falsifiable targets** (measured in playtests): a playtester describes the breach as "my base got tested," never "the game switched screens"; prepared and unprepared players report different emotions at the identical freeze frame (**scoped to players past their first raid** — a first-time player has no trained read; their onboarding channel is the pre-raid CD-15 warning, which is colony-side and outside Rule 10's no-pop-up scope); observed 3× usage rises as a colony matures (**maturity axis: in-game days elapsed** — measured as time-at-speed telemetry binned by game-day); no playtester asks whether battle damage "carries over." The "direct command of individual people" claim above is verified by the Combat set's (#19–23) own AC set — named here so the claim's verification owner is on record (re-review 2026-08-02).

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

1. **Two temporal models, one world.** Exactly one time authority is active at any moment: continuous real time (colony) or discrete turns (combat). The inactive side receives nothing — the colony fully pauses during a battle, and combat systems are dormant during colony play. There is no partial simulation, ever. Full suspension is not a Pillar 4 concession: the freeze renders the colony's work *maximally* visible — a held tableau of every hauler and mason mid-task (the view-freeze contract, AC-64, exists precisely to keep that work on screen) — rather than hiding it behind an abstraction.
2. **Only the game switches modes.** Raid Trigger (#18) is the sole requester of the switch into combat; the Combat set (#19–23) is the sole requester of the switch back. The player has no manual "enter combat" verb in MVP. Any future feature wanting to trigger a switch (training, duels, scripted battles) is a new design decision revising this rule — never silent reuse.
3. **Speed control is a colony-only concept.** Pause/1×/2×/3×, where speed multiplies the simulation's fixed-step count and never changes step size. The speed UI shows the **requested** multiplier; on a heavy colony the sim may deliver less than 3× — sustained pace degrades gracefully and **dropped time is never re-simulated later** (no catch-up: a single stalled dispatch may still process up to `SubStepCap` sub-steps of bounded compensation, which is not a "burst" in this rule's sense — see Formula D.1's backlog semantics). Whether a frame *hitches* under aggregate load is the frame-budget ledger's concern (OQ #8), not a guarantee this rule can make — this rule guarantees only that the throttling mechanism itself never death-spirals. During combat the dial is **hidden**, replaced by combat UI — turn pacing is Combat's concern and is not driven by the colony speed dial. **Obligation transmitted to the Combat set (CD-GDD-ALIGN C3)**: it must own a pacing/animation-speed control of its own (a presentation knob, distinct from this dial — also an accessibility need), or the 8–15 minute battle budget is unreachable with every lever removed. **Obligation transmitted to the HUD spec (#26/shared shell, re-review 2026-08-02)**: some delivered-vs-requested signal MUST exist when the sim under-delivers — Player Fantasy stakes the dial as an honest confidence readout, and silent divergence corrodes it; the signal's form is the HUD spec's call (same transmission pattern as C3).
4. **A raid cannot fire while paused — by construction.** Threat accumulates only inside real-time simulation steps; at speed 0 there are none. No special case exists or is needed.
5. **CD-10 at the boundary, as UI availability**: during combat the player may *view* everything but *author* nothing — blueprint, dig, build, zone, crafting, and hauling orders are unavailable and inert (not merely hidden; presentation owned by #26). Combat may change world state only through the existing writer surfaces under the combat-mode writer set (ADR-0002/0003) — no mutation category may exist that is exclusive to combat. **Activation is legal change, not authoring (CD-11)**: spending an action to operate a pre-built object — doors, levers, drop-gates — realizes value built in peacetime and is the one Expression-adjacent verb available inside combat; activation cost is Action Economy's (#20). *Forward note (CD-12, Vertical Slice)*: colonist-carried deployables are sanctioned at VS and may need a combat-scoped placement writer — that is a planned revision of this rule's no-combat-exclusive-category clause, not a violation discovered late.
6. **One encounter at a time.** A second trigger while a battle is pending or active is rejected, never queued. Simultaneous multi-point breaches are one encounter with multiple breach cells, never two battles. What Raid Trigger does with a rejected trigger (reschedule, re-accumulate) is #18's decision — flagged as a required cross-reference.
7. **Battle end is Combat's call; the exit is ours.** Combat decides when the battle is over and who won. Every exit — victory, defeat, raider withdrawal, and any future player retreat (existence deferred to the Combat set) — funnels through the **identical** switch-back and reconcile path. No lighter exit exists.
8. **The canonical return sequence**, in order: battle resolves → authority swaps → `PostEncounterReconcile` runs (outcome report dispatched, dead colonists' reservations released, orphaned jobs/paths cancelled, dead/broken/withdrawn entities reaped) → **colony-time catch-up runs (CD-19, user ruling 2026-08-07)** → battle-end autosave → **speed forced to 0** regardless of pre-battle speed → after-action survey presented (Combat UI, CD-1) → player regains the dial. World state resumes exactly; speed does not. **The catch-up step (CD-19)** advances colony time by a turn-derived, capped amount — ordinary RealTime `Tick()` sub-steps, run once, immediately, exactly like any other colony dispatch — placed strictly *after* reconcile (so it never touches an encounter-era side table) and strictly *before* the battle-end autosave (so a save never holds pending time debt). The rate, the cap, and the guardrails (multiplicative-decay-only, the clemency clause against crossing a lethal threshold in one injection, dead-colonists-reaped-first, a single Job Assignment re-plan) are Needs & Simulation's (#13) tuning knobs and rules — this GDD owns only that the step exists and where it sits in the sequence. **The survey's data source is pinned (CD-GDD-ALIGN M2)**: the survey reads the drained `EncounterOutcomeReport` — the **only guaranteed carrier** of encounter-era data across reconcile, since encounter-scoped side tables are torn down by the time the survey renders. The breach log rides that report per the 2026-07-26 CD ruling assigning its ownership to Combat: Targeting & Resolution (#22); nothing the survey needs may live only in reaped side tables.
9. **Three silent autosave moments; manual saves locked from the raid warning through battle-end, not merely "in combat" (Battle Persistence 2026-08-02, extended by CD-20 2026-08-07).** (a) At switch-in: captures the **pre-swap colony state**, sequenced strictly before the swap. (b) **During battle: one rolling checkpoint, written after each actor activation resolves** — a single non-selectable slot, tagged `Mode == TurnBased`, and the *only* legal writer of a combat-mode save. **The first checkpoint ("activation 0") is written immediately post-swap, before the first activation** (ADR-0004): without it, the newest save during squad placement is the RealTime switch-in autosave and quitting there would land pre-battle — the exact window this rule closes. Loading always resumes from the **latest** autosave, so a mid-battle relaunch lands at the start of the activation after the last resolved one: combat outcomes, once resolved, cannot be avoided by reloading, and no in-battle scum window exists because the checkpoint is post-resolution. (c) At battle end: **after** reconcile completes and **after** the CD-19 catch-up step, so the save never contains orphaned state or pending time debt. No player save-slot interaction at any of the three moments.

    **The manual save control is disabled — visibly inert, not hidden — from the CD-15 raid-warning notification through battle-end** (CD-20, widening CD-GDD-ALIGN C5's in-combat-only scope): the player never authors a save across the whole raid window, only the checkpoint/autosave system does; a TurnBased-tagged save from any writer but the checkpoint remains corrupt by definition. Quitting during the warning window (still RealTime — no encounter exists yet) is unaffected by the checkpoint mechanism and covered by the ordinary colony autosave rotation, same as any other colony-mode quit — this window is deliberately **not** hardened into a scum-proof lock the way in-battle is, because it cannot be: a colony save from before the warning always exists somewhere in the rotation or a manual slot the player made earlier.

    **What closes the gap instead is cost, not prohibition (CD-20)**: loading *any* colony save older than the current raid — manual or autosave — after that raid has resolved is a legal player action, but it re-derives the raid's breach point and composition from the seeded RNG stream at that point rather than replaying the one that already happened (Raid Trigger #18 + ADR-0005, within CD-15's information ceiling). A rewind retakes a different test; it never replays a known answer key. Players who want the stricter promise — no colony-save rewind at all — can start a colony in **Commitment Mode**: a per-colony, immutable-after-creation toggle (default **off**) that removes manual saves and the older-save load option entirely, leaving one rolling save (full UX owned by Save/Load #6). *(This is CD-20's honest replacement for the "no pre-battle rewind by any player action" absolutism EC-8 previously claimed — see Player Fantasy and EC-8/EC-12.)*

    *(Battle Persistence overturned CD-9's no-mid-battle-save half — ADR-0001/0003 and the contracts annex carry that propagation via `/propagate-design-change`; CD-9's battle-length half stands. CD-20 is a second, later propagation addressing the colony-level gap Battle Persistence left open.)*
10. **Notifications queue across the boundary.** Anything generated during combat queues silently and presents only after return to colony play. No pop-ups interrupt a battle.
11. **Menu pause is not a game mode.** A settings/menu overlay is presentation-only, on an independent axis from both the speed dial and the turn state. Closing it returns to exactly the prior speed or mid-turn state — nothing skipped, nothing double-applied. It never becomes a third temporal model and never touches the simulation path.
12. **Orders in flight at the freeze are dropped silently.** A colony order submitted in the same instant the switch lands is discarded with no world effect and no error — the window is sub-frame and imperceptible.
13. **Two kinds of "frozen," named for triage**: at speed 0 the colony systems are *active but stepping zero times*; during combat they are *inactive and receive nothing*. Identical on screen, structurally different — a bug in one is not a bug in the other.
14. **The default registration assumption**: colony systems freeze during battle unless their own spec says otherwise. Every simulation spec carries a "Behavior under each time authority" section written against ADR-0001's worked-example table; passive stores declare *inert; only the legal writer set changes*.

## Formulas

Two formulas govern this system. Both restate ADR-0001's sub-stepping contract in design-consumable form; the shared values (`SubStepCap`, `SubStepDuration`, `EngineFrameRate`, `GameSecondsPerGameDay`) are defined once in Tuning Knobs. Deliberately **not** formulas: the fixed dt (a pinned tuning knob with nothing to compute), the CD-9 battle-length budget (a threshold — see Acceptance Criteria), and the `TurnIndex`/`TickSequence` counters (fixed increment rules, ADR-0001's concern).

### Formula D.1 — Delivered Sub-Steps Per Physics Frame

`SubStepsDelivered = min(SubStepsRequested, SubStepCap)`

| Variable | Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Requested sub-steps | SubStepsRequested | int | 0–unbounded (practically small) | Sub-steps the active speed multiplier plus any real-frame catch-up backlog ask for this dispatch |
| Sub-step cap | SubStepCap | int | ≥1 (default 8) | Per-frame ceiling; the lower of the engine clamp and our own configured cap |
| Delivered sub-steps | SubStepsDelivered | int | 0–SubStepCap | Sub-steps that actually run — determines how much game time really advances |

**Output Range:** 0 (paused) to SubStepCap; never negative, never exceeds the cap regardless of request size — this is what makes "the sim slows down, never death-spirals" a guaranteed property rather than a hope.
**Example (normal frame):** speed 3×, on-schedule frame: `min(3, 8) = 3` — no throttling; MVP's max speed has headroom.
**Example (stalled frame):** the engine catches up 4 frames' worth at 3×: `min(12, 8) = 8` → the player experiences ~67% of the requested pace for that moment — a visible slowdown, not a skip or a hang. This is the mechanism behind Core Rule 3's "silently delivers less than 3×."

**Backlog semantics — the accumulator behind `SubStepsRequested` (design review 2026-08-02)**: the RealTime authority owns a single time accumulator (it lives in `RealTimeAuthority` per ADR-0001; no other copy exists). Each physics frame adds `engineDelta × speed` to it, and `SubStepsRequested = floor(accumulator ÷ SubStepDuration)`. After Formula D.1 runs, `SubStepsDelivered × SubStepDuration` is consumed from the accumulator — and any *whole-step* time beyond the cap is **discarded with it** (this is AC-4's "backlog is dropped, not queued" and Rule 3's "never catches up in a burst": the lost time is simply never simulated; the sim slows). The *sub-step residual* (< 1 × `SubStepDuration`) is **never discarded** — it persists in the accumulator across frames and across a mode freeze (EC-9). The boundary is exact: time ≥ one whole sub-step beyond the cap → dropped; time < one sub-step → preserved. Rule 3, AC-4, and EC-9 describe this one mechanism, not three policies.

### Formula D.2 — Colony Time Elapsed Per Real-Time Second

`GameSecondsPerRealSecond = SubStepsDelivered × SubStepDuration × EngineFrameRate`

| Variable | Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Delivered sub-steps | SubStepsDelivered | int | 0–SubStepCap | Output of Formula D.1 |
| Sub-step duration | SubStepDuration | float (s) | fixed (default 1/60 s) | The fixed dt every sub-step advances by; never scaled |
| Engine physics frame rate | EngineFrameRate | float (Hz) | fixed (default 60 Hz) | Physics dispatch rate under unstalled conditions |
| Result | GameSecondsPerRealSecond | float | 0–SubStepCap (while SubStepDuration × EngineFrameRate = 1; 8 at defaults) | Simulated seconds per real second at the current speed |

**Output Range:** 0 at pause; the ceiling equals `SubStepCap` game-seconds/real-second whenever `SubStepDuration × EngineFrameRate = 1` (8 at defaults) — MVP speeds (max 3×) never hit it under normal frame timing. That product staying exactly 1 is **asserted at startup** (AC-63): the two values live in different config locations (engine setting vs. gameplay constant), and D.2 silently degrades to a lie if they ever diverge.
**Example:** at 3×: `3 × (1/60) × 60 = 3` game-seconds per real second. **A game-second is a simulation unit, not a fictional second** — the fiction-to-simulation compression lives entirely in the `GameSecondsPerGameDay` tuning knob (**1440** — 1 game-hour of fiction = 60 game-seconds of simulation; user decision at design review 2026-08-02). Derivation: a 24-game-hour day = `GameSecondsPerGameDay ÷ GameSecondsPerRealSecond` = `1440 ÷ 3` = 480 real seconds = **8 real minutes at 3×**, 24 at 1×. The canonical citation for downstream systems is this **pair** — the D.2 conversion rate *and* `GameSecondsPerGameDay` — which Needs decay, day/night, and crafting durations must cite rather than re-derive (registered in the entity registry on approval of this GDD). Citing the rate without the day-length constant reproduces the 60× ambiguity this revision closed.

**Two citable forms (re-review 2026-08-02, B7)**: the divisive form above (`GameSecondsPerGameDay ÷ GameSecondsPerRealSecond`) is **presentation-only and undefined at pause** — at speed 0, `GameSecondsPerRealSecond = 0` and the division is meaningless; semantically correct (a paused day never ends), but any HUD countdown or readout citing it MUST guard the zero case. Systems integrating progress use the **multiplicative form, which never divides by the rate**:

`DayProgressThisDispatch = (SubStepsDelivered × SubStepDuration) ÷ GameSecondsPerGameDay`

— equivalently, a per-day rate applies as `AmountThisDispatch = RatePerDay × (SubStepsDelivered × SubStepDuration) ÷ GameSecondsPerGameDay`. At pause it correctly evaluates to zero progress. This is the form Needs decay, day/night, and crafting durations cite; the divisive form is for readouts only.

## Edge Cases

- **If Raid Trigger's threshold is reached while speed is 0**: impossible by construction — threat accumulates only inside real-time steps, and speed 0 delivers none. No "check while paused" path exists. *(EC-1)*
- **If a second breach condition is met while an encounter is active or pending**: the switch request is rejected silently — no visible transition, no queued second battle. Raid Trigger (#18) owns what becomes of the rejected threshold (reset / hold / re-arm). *(EC-2)*
- **If a UI action is mid-authoring at the freeze** (uncommitted drag-select, unconfirmed blueprint, open zone-menu selection): dropped silently, never resumed after battle — the referenced state may no longer exist by then. **Scope**: this covers *uncommitted player input* only; a committed job (a colonist mid-haul) simply freezes with its owner and resumes correctly. (#26 may choose to preserve *tool state* — active tool, brush size — across the battle even though the selection is dropped; its call.) **Presentation obligation on #26 (design review 2026-08-02)**: the drop must resolve visually *within* the freeze-transition beat — the authoring chrome (blueprint ghost, drag rectangle, open menu) clears as part of the same ~2-second freeze the player is already reading, never before it and never after it as a second unexplained event. "Dropped silently" is a data guarantee, not a licence for an unacknowledged vanish — outside the beat, it reads as a bug. *(EC-3)*
- **If a menu overlay is open when the switch is accepted**: the switch proceeds regardless (axis independence, Rule 11); the menu is force-closed on entering combat, since CD-10-restricted colony UI has no combat equivalent. *(EC-4)*
- **If the battle ends with zero surviving defenders**: the return path is identical — reconcile reaps all the dead with no "all vs. some" branch, autosave fires, speed forces to 0, the survey displays. The survey camera, with no surviving *defender* to frame (undrafted colonists may well survive elsewhere in the colony — "zero defenders" is not "zero colonists"), defaults to the **breach site / colony overview**. Whether total loss triggers any further failure state is the Combat set's / game concept's domain — this system's contract is satisfied identically. *(EC-5)*
- **If the player issues a manual save in the same frame the switch is accepted** (pending, not yet applied): the save is allowed and completes normally — the mode is still real-time for the entire remainder of that dispatch; the swap is atomic between dispatches, so the manual-save mode rule (Rule 9) is never at risk. *(EC-6, regression-test candidate)*
- **If the player changes speed in the same frame the switch is accepted**: honored for the remainder of that dispatch (harmless — the view cuts before the next rendered frame), then irrelevant: return always forces speed 0. *(EC-7)*
- **If the player quits to desktop mid-battle**: on relaunch they load the **latest battle checkpoint** (Rule 9b) — the battle resumes at the start of the activation following the last resolved one. No rewind to pre-battle exists via *this* action (Battle Persistence, user ruling 2026-08-02); the switch-in autosave (Rule 9a) remains the sequencing anchor but is never the newest save while a battle is live. A **confirmation dialog** states the behavior: *"Quitting suspends the battle — it will resume exactly where you left off."* Deterministic resumption requires the checkpoint to carry the combat RNG stream state — **obligation transmitted to Save/Load (#6) and the Seeded RNG ADR**. *(This retires the prior quit-rewind design debt (CD-GDD-ALIGN M1), the reload seed-policy question (old OQ #3a — nothing is re-rolled; the same battle continues), and the suspend-to-exit upgrade path: Battle Persistence is suspend-to-exit generalized to every quit and shipped as foundation. It also closes both exploits found at re-review — the reload-then-pause re-decision window and the re-roll slot machine — at the root, since **quitting** can no longer reach the pre-battle moment. **Correction (CD-20, 2026-08-07)**: a *different* player action — loading an older colony save, not quitting — still can reach it; that gap is Rule 9's raid-warning save lock plus the reload-re-roll obligation, not this edge case. See EC-12.)* *(EC-8)*
- **If a sub-frame time backlog (<1 sub-step) exists at the freeze**: preserved, not discarded — the inactive authority's accumulator sits inert and resumes exactly where it left off. No observable effect; stated so nobody "fixes" it by resetting the accumulator on swap. *(EC-9)*
- **If Squad Prep drafts zero colonists when a raid fires** (all dead, incapacitated, or unreachable): **the switch proceeds anyway** — an empty-roster encounter is legal, and Combat resolves the unopposed raid inside turn-based mode (user decision 2026-08-02, chosen over a Raid-Trigger pre-check). **Obligation transmitted to the Combat set (#19–23)**: its turn loop must handle zero player-controlled units — raiders act, the player observes; the identical exit path still applies. **CD constraint (CD-GDD-ALIGN C6, hardened at design review 2026-08-02)**: an unopposed encounter is a consequence being applied, not a test — the Combat set **MUST bound a zero-player-unit encounter to a short non-interactive resolution** (fast-resolve or skip; the *mechanism* is Combat's call under Rule 7, and either satisfies the identical exit path). The player must not be made to watch up to 20 minutes of zero-agency raiding — as a MAY, this protection could be cut under scope pressure; it is now an obligation this GDD transmits, not an option it permits. CD-3's survivability floor remains #18's to define — this edge case is not a licence to skip it. *(EC-10)*
- **If the player leaves speed at 0 after a battle and never resumes**: no new raid can ever trigger (same mechanism as EC-1). Stated as a deliberate design property, not an accident: resume-at-pause doubles as a safety buffer — the player is never ambushed again until they choose to restart time. *(EC-11)*
- **If the player loads a colony save (manual or autosave) that predates a raid which has since resolved**: legal — this is the gap EC-8's checkpoint machinery does not close, because it isn't trying to (CD-20, user ruling 2026-08-07). Battle Persistence guarantees only that the *fight* cannot be rewound once it begins; the colony's save history before it still can be. The reload is not free: the raid that follows re-derives its breach point and composition from the seeded RNG stream at that point (Raid Trigger #18 + the Seeded RNG ADR) rather than replaying the raid that already happened, within CD-15's information ceiling — a rewind retakes a different test, never the same one twice. Loading a save from *inside* the raid-warning-through-battle-end window is not player-reachable in the first place (Rule 9's lock); this edge case covers only saves from before that window opened. Commitment Mode colonies cannot reach this edge case at all — no older-save load option exists for them. *(EC-12)*

**Evaluated and excluded** (tuning-knob constraints, not runtime edges): `SubStepCap` below the max speed multiplier (safe-range note in Tuning Knobs); physics tick rate changes (build-time concern; ADR-0001 requires revisiting the ADR itself).

## Tuning Knobs

| Knob | Default | Safe range | What it affects / what breaks at extremes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speed multiplier set | {0, 1, 2, 3} | any integers with max ≤ `SubStepCap` | The colony speed options. Adding 4× touches zero simulation systems (spike-proven) but must stay ≤ `SubStepCap` or the new speed silently never delivers its multiplier (permanent degradation, not load-dependent); each step up multiplies per-frame sim cost linearly |
| `SubStepCap` | 8 | max speed multiplier … 8 | Per-frame sub-step ceiling (Formula D.1). Too low: high speeds permanently degraded even on light colonies. Too high: a slow frame does more catch-up work, worsening the very frame pacing that caused it — the cap exists to make the sim slow down gracefully. (Top of range narrowed from ~12 at re-review 2026-08-02: values above 8 sit squarely in the zone this cell's own warning describes) |
| `SubStepDuration` (fixed dt) | 1/60 s | **do not treat as a lever** | Advances per sub-step; every rate constant in every system is calibrated against it. Changing it changes simulation-speed semantics project-wide and **requires revisiting ADR-0001** — a build-time decision, never a balance pass |
| `EngineFrameRate` (physics tick) | 60 Hz | **do not treat as a lever** | Pinned in project settings; same warning as dt. The pair (dt, tick rate) defines Formula D.2's real-time↔game-time mapping |
| Battle length budget | 8–15 min target, 20 min hard ceiling | ceiling 15–25 min | CD-9's single-sitting integrity (its surviving half). Owned here as the *boundary budget*; the Combat set owns achieving it (its pacing levers live there). Raising the ceiling dilutes the single-sitting intent — since Battle Persistence, the budget's justification is pacing and Pillar 2 legibility, not save absence; lowering the target below ~8 min makes battles too small to express Pillar 2 |
| `GameSecondsPerGameDay` | 1440 (1 game-hour = 60 game-seconds) | 960–2880 (16–48 real min/day at 1×; **validate any retune at the highest shipped speed, not 1×** — the floor at 3× is a 5.3-real-minute day, inside this cell's own "too low" failure zone) | The fiction-to-simulation day compression — the second half of the canonical pair with Formula D.2 (user decision, design review 2026-08-02). Every system expressing rates "per day/hour" converts through this knob only. Too low: day/night and raid cadence churn faster than the build loop can answer; too high: sessions span fractions of a day and the "one more room" pull loses its day-rhythm anchor |

**Knob interactions**: Formula D.2's output has exactly **one** real lever — `SubStepsDelivered` (speed × cap) — because AC-63 pins `SubStepDuration × EngineFrameRate = 1` at startup: those two are a fixed unit-conversion pair, not tuning levers, and retuning either alone fails the config-load guard (they move only together, as a build-time decision revisiting ADR-0001). Max speed and `SubStepCap` form a hard constraint pair (see safe ranges). **Anti-duplication rule**: downstream systems must not create their own real-time↔game-time conversion knobs — Needs decay, day/night, and crafting durations cite the canonical **pair** (Formula D.2's `GameSecondsPerRealSecond` + `GameSecondsPerGameDay`) as the single source of truth; neither half alone is citable.

**Config-load guards (design review 2026-08-02, AC-62/63)**: three assertions run at startup, before the first dispatch. (1) `SubStepCap ≥ 1` **and** `SubStepCap ≥` max speed multiplier — a cap of 0 is a silent total simulation freeze at every speed (raids can never fire, EC-1's mechanism inverted into a bug) while the UI still reads 3×; the tuning table's low-end warning covers new speeds *above* the cap but nothing else catches a misconfigured cap itself. (2) `SubStepDuration == 1 / EngineFrameRate` (±1e-9) — D.2's ceiling silently depends on this product being 1. (3) The engine's own physics-steps-per-frame clamp, **read from the real project setting at runtime, never hardcoded**, must be ≥ `SubStepCap` — the curated engine-reference docs stop at 4.6 and the 4.7.1 setting name/default is unverified (routed to technical-director, OQ #9), so the code must compare against what the engine actually reports.

## Visual/Audio Requirements

This system owns no assets, but it owns the constraint governing the game's single most important presentation beat. Requirements transmitted to their owners:

- **The freeze is a decompression, not a bang** (creative-director adjudication, 2026-08-02): ambient colony bustle drops to silence; motion stops; no stinger, no screen shake, no adrenal spike. A jump-scare transition would inject reflex-game sensation into the one beat that must read as *judgment* — silence is the sound of an exam hall. **Owner: audio-director** (the silence drop and the ambient bed it is measured against), **art-director** (freeze-frame readability). *(AC-47)*
- **Freeze legibility within ~2 seconds**: the player must locate the breach and read their own preparedness off the frame — camera settles on the breach point; the colony's held motion stays visible (colonists caught mid-stride, not despawned). **Owner: art-director + Combat UI (#27)**.
- **The view-freeze contract (named here, design review 2026-08-02; implementation owned by the view layer)**: "held motion stays visible" is not free. Under ADR-0001, `_Process` is presentation-only and *keeps running* after `Tick()` stops — a view driving its own tweens, `AnimationPlayer` playback, or procedural motion would keep animating straight through the freeze, and one interpolating from tick outputs would snap rather than hold. The sanctioned freeze signal is the **`ModeTransitioned` direct event** (ADR-0001): on the RealTime→TurnBased transition, every presentation node stops advancing self-driven animation state (interpolation alpha, tween/animation playback, procedural sway) and **holds its current pose**; on the return transition it resumes. This is distinct from `IPresentationGate`, which paces combat-interior turn advancement only. **Owner: Terrain Rendering & Cutaway (#7) + colonist views (ADR-0003)**; flagged to technical-director as a required companion note on ADR-0001's presentation layer. *(AC-64)*
- **The return beat**: the colony view restores at pause over the damage — no triumphant sting on victory, no failure sting on defeat; the survey (CD-1) carries the verdict. **Owner: audio-director + #27**.
- Speed-change feedback (dial clicks, time-lapse audio texture at 2×/3×) is cosmetic and owned by the UI/audio pass — no gameplay semantics (lighting/audio never signal threat state, per the art bible's aesthetic-only rule).

Consolidated from Core Rules for the three UX specs; this GDD owns the *rules*, the specs own the presentation:

- **Speed dial** (colony HUD, all three specs reference it): displays requested multiplier only (Rule 3); hidden during combat, replaced by combat UI (#27); reads 0 on every return from battle (Rule 8).
- **Order inertness in combat** (#26): all six order categories visibly disabled or absent — not merely unresponsive — with no queuing (Rule 5, AC-40). What the player sees when they try is #26's UX-spec decision (CD-10 requires it be *inert, not hidden* at minimum).
- **Quit-mid-battle confirmation** (#27 or shared shell UI): *"Quitting suspends the battle — it will resume exactly where you left off"* — blocking, behavior-stating dialog, combat mode only (EC-8, AC-43; re-authored at re-review 2026-08-02 under Battle Persistence). This string is the canonical constant **`QuitConfirmText`** — UI renders it verbatim, and AC-43 checks equality against the constant, not resemblance. **The affirmative (quit) option must NOT hold default focus** — default focus and the Enter/confirm key map to staying in the battle, so a stressed player cannot key-repeat through the dialog by accident (re-review 2026-08-02).
- **Manual save control** (shared shell): visibly disabled — inert, not hidden, with a labelled reason — from the raid-warning notification through battle-end (widened from combat-only at CD-20, 2026-08-07); re-enabled on return (Rule 9, CD-GDD-ALIGN C5, CD-20, AC-44/AC-71). **Load-list labelling** (shared shell, CD-20): the load screen marks each save with its in-game date and a "before raid #N" indicator where applicable, so loading an older save is a visible, deliberate act, not an accidental one. **Commitment Mode** (new-game setup, CD-20): a per-colony toggle, default off, immutable after creation — full UX owned by Save/Load #6.
- **After-action survey** (#27, CD-1): presented at the forced pause before the player regains the dial (Rule 8); camera fallback to breach site on total loss (EC-5). **Focus loss** (alt-tab, window minimize) while the survey is displayed is safe by construction — the sim is already at forced speed 0 — so the survey simply persists until answered, with no timeout and no escape that skips it (design review 2026-08-02). **Bound on #27 (re-review 2026-08-02, B10)**: the survey must be clearable by a **single unambiguous confirm action** — additional detail may sit behind it, but is never required to clear it. The no-escape wall is only safe because the way through it is guaranteed trivial.
- **Disabled-state consistency** (#26/#27/shared shell): all six order-category controls — whatever their control type (toolbar button, drag gesture, menu item) — share **one** disabled-state visual treatment, legible without color alone. #26 owns what the treatment is; this GDD owns that there is exactly one (design review 2026-08-02). **Gesture-control exception (re-review 2026-08-02, U2)**: a continuous gesture (drag-select) has no at-rest surface to carry a disabled treatment, and "no rectangle spawns" is exactly the banned merely-unresponsive outcome — for gesture-type categories #26 MUST provide an **on-attempt transient affordance** (cursor badge, struck ghost-rectangle, or equivalent); the shared-treatment rule applies to its visual language, not its idle presentation.
- **Accessibility cross-reference**: this document's zero-agency interruption rule (the freeze can land mid-input with no grace window), the inert-not-hidden rule, the quit-dialog, **and the after-action survey's no-timeout/no-escape persistence rule — this document's single highest lockout risk, whose single-confirm clearability bound (above) is the mitigation floor** — are **binding inputs** to `design/ux/accessibility-requirements.md` (not yet written) — that document owns the mitigations (e.g., color-independent disabled states, readability of the freeze beat) and may not relax the rules themselves (design review 2026-08-02).
- **Menu overlay** (shared shell): force-closes on switch-in (EC-4); never affects sim or turn state (Rule 11).
- **Notifications** (shared component): queued during combat, flushed on return (Rule 10) — presentation of the flush (burst vs. digest) is the component's spec decision.

### (b) Performance — headless benchmark harness (BLOCKING regression gate; tolerance bands above measured, allocation gate tight per Terrain precedent)

**Methodology preconditions (design review 2026-08-02)**: the spike's baselines are single-run Stopwatch measurements — a sample of one cannot size a tolerance band. Re-baseline, don't silently raise, if the cause is legitimate scope growth.
35. **[Swap cost, measured 0.31 µs]** GIVEN a RealTime→TurnBased swap under reference load, WHEN benchmarked over ≥1,000 swaps, THEN mean cost ≤ **0.40 µs** (measured + ~29% — wider band, sub-microsecond timings are more timer-noise-sensitive).
36. **[Reconcile cost, measured 28.9 µs — two-point scaling gate per design review 2026-08-02]** GIVEN the reference reconcile load (50 jobs, 50 paths, 20 reservations, 1 death, 3 raiders) **and a 4× load** (200 jobs, 200 paths, 80 reservations, 4 deaths, 12 raiders), WHEN each is benchmarked over ≥1,000 passes, THEN (a) reference mean cost ≤ **35 µs** (measured + ~21%), AND (b) `mean(4×) ÷ mean(reference) ≤ 6` — near-linear growth (an O(n²) reconcile would ratio ~16). The gate exists to catch an accidental O(n²) as content scales; a single fixed-size point cannot distinguish O(n) from O(n²) — any monotonic curve fits one point — which is why the ratio clause, not the absolute number, is the load-bearing half. **Raider-axis clause (re-review 2026-08-02, P3)**: a uniform multiplier can mask a superlinear term in a swamped mix — an O(jobs) + O(raiders²) reconcile scores ratio ≈5.8 on the 4× point, *passing* the ≤6 gate while quadratic — so add a third point scaling **only raiders** (reference jobs/paths/reservations, raiders 3→24) with its own near-linear gate (≤ 8×); final load points defer to Raid Trigger (#18)'s real wave sizes once authored. **Allocation clause (re-review 2026-08-02, P4)**: over the same ≥1,000 passes, reconcile allocation is measured and **Gen0 == 0 per pass** — reconcile is once-per-battle (not steady-state, so AC-37's per-sub-step gate structurally cannot cover it) but is the likeliest silent-allocation site (list-building, LINQ over reaped entities) and lands immediately before the presentation-critical return beat.
